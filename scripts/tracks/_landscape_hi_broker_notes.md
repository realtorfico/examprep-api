## CONFIRMED FACT 4: Exam mechanics (from PSI Candidate Information Bulletin, "Examination Summary Table")
BROKER exam:
- Uniform (National/General) portion: 75 scored items (worth 80 points — see note on 2-pt scoring below), 150 minutes
- State (Hawaii) portion: 50 items (50 points), 90 minutes
- Combo (both portions together): 125 items (130 points total), 240 minutes
- Passing score: 75% for Brokers (stated as a flat percentage in the bulletin's "SCORE REPORTING" section: "The passing scores (percentages) are as follows: Brokers: 75% / Salespersons: 70% / Instructors: 85%")
- Note in bulletin: "National broker exams include questions that are scored up to two points" — i.e. some broker Uniform-portion items are worth 2 points each rather than 1, which is why 75 items = 80 points (not a simple item-count-only weighting). This is a real, documented scoring quirk unique to the broker exam — worth flagging for question-writing since it affects how "one item = one point" assumptions should NOT be made for the broker uniform portion.
- Sections are separately timed AND the bulletin explicitly allows retaking only the failed section (uniform or state) within 2 years of the date the other section was passed — so uniform and state are scored/passed independently, not combined into one composite pass/fail.
- Broker Equivalency (State only, for candidates who hold reciprocal/equivalent uniform-section credit): 50 items, 90 minutes.
- Experimental/unscored questions: 5-10 additional unscored questions may appear per exam (count against time, not counted toward score).
For comparison, SALESPERSON exam: Uniform 80 items (80 pts)/150 min; State 50 items (50 pts)/90 min; Combo 130 items (130 pts)/240 min; passing score 70%.
Fee: "$61 for the examination, regardless if you take one (1) or both examination portions." (Broker and Salesperson Examination Fee — same $61 fee for either license type.) Non-refundable, valid 1 year from date of payment.

## CONFIRMED FACT 9: Real Estate Recovery Fund cap = $25,000 per transaction
Source: Official Hawaii Real Estate Commission memorandum "Claim from the Real Estate Recovery Fund",
downloaded directly from https://cca.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/recov.pdf (linked from
https://cca.hawaii.gov/reb/resources-for-real-estate-licensees/ under "Information on the Real Estate
Recovery Fund"). Exact quote: "...may recover upon the Commission's settlement of a claim or by order
of the circuit court or district court of the county where the violation occurred, an amount of not
more than $25,000 per transaction. No action for a judgment which subsequently results in a court
order for collection from the real estate recovery fund shall be started later than two years from
the accrual of the cause of action thereon." Grounds: fraud, misrepresentation, or deceit by a
licensed broker or salesperson. (Note: this document's letterhead names an older Governor/Director,
i.e. the memo itself is an older undated-by-year source republished on the current site — the $25,000
figure and 2-year statute of limitations should be treated as the figure currently published by REB,
but flagging that the memo's date is not visible in the extracted text in case a newer aggregate-cap
figure exists elsewhere; did not find a separate "aggregate/per-licensee" cap figure distinct from the
per-transaction figure in this document.)

## CONFIRMED FACT 10: Recovery Fund — precise statutory figures (superseding Fact 9's memo-based figure; statute is authoritative)
Source: HRS Chapter 467 full text, downloaded directly as PDF from https://cca.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/467-REAL-ESTATE-BROKERS-AND-SALESPERSONS-FINAL-101420-1.pdf
(unofficial copy hosted by REB, "Revised: 07-2020"; cross-checked against the official Hawaii
Capitol text link on the same REB page for the current codified chapter). Both pdftotext default and
-layout extraction modes used (columnar PDF renders better with -layout).
- HRS §467-16, "Real estate recovery fund; use of fund; fees" (a): recovery cap = "an amount of not
  more than $25,000 per transaction for damages sustained by the fraud, misrepresentation, or deceit,
  including court costs and fees as set by law, and reasonable attorney fees as determined by the court."
  Grounds: fraud, misrepresentation, or deceit by a licensed broker/salesperson, via Commission
  settlement or court order.
- HRS §467-24, "Maximum liability": "the liability of the real estate recovery fund shall not exceed
  $50,000 for any one licensee." => So $25,000 is the PER-TRANSACTION cap and $50,000 is the
  AGGREGATE cap per licensee across claims (these are complementary, not conflicting, figures).
- HRS §467-17, "Additional payments to fund": if fund balance falls below $350,000, DCCA must assess
  every licensee an additional payment to replenish it (nonpayment within 60 days = license forfeiture).
- HRS §467-18: 2-year statute of limitations to commence an action that could result in fund recovery.
- HRS §467-26, "Penalty": violating HRS Chapter 467 is punishable by a fine of "not more than $5,000
  for each violation."
- Note: the memo at recov.pdf (Fact 9) only states the $25,000 per-transaction figure and omits the
  $50,000 aggregate cap — both are real, current statutory numbers per the HRS 467 text itself; use
  both, correctly distinguished, in question-writing.

## CONFIRMED FACT 11: Broker experience prerequisite (exact statutory language)
Source: HRS §467-9.5(a)(4)(B), same HRS Chapter 467 PDF as above (layout-mode extraction).
To sit the Broker examination, in addition to citizenship/age/HS-diploma requirements shared with
Salesperson candidates, an applicant must:
(i) hold a current, unencumbered Hawaii real estate salesperson license (or equivalent out-of-state
    license per an ARELLO-recognized jurisdiction determination by the Commission);
(ii) have satisfactorily completed a Commission-approved broker prelicensing course (or equivalent);
(iii) "Has experience as a full-time Hawaii-licensed real estate salesperson associated with a
     Hawaii-licensed real estate broker for at least three years of the five-year period immediately
     prior to the submission of the experience certification application" with practical experience
     certified by the principal broker(s)/broker(s)-in-charge during that period. This Experience
     Certification Application must be Commission-approved BEFORE the candidate can sit the broker exam
     (matches the PSI bulletin's "Experience Certificate for Real Estate Broker's Examination" requirement
     confirmed in Fact 2/4).
=> Exact numeric rule for question-writing: 3 years of full-time licensed salesperson experience within
the most recent 5-year window (not simply "3 years licensed").

## CONFIRMED FACT 12: Trust account rules (HAR §16-99-4)
Source: HAR Chapter 99 PDF, https://cca.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/99-C.pdf (pdftotext
-layout; note: pdftotext threw "Unknown filter 'Crypt'" on some embedded objects but body text
extracted fine). Confirmed provision cite: §16-99-4.
- (d) "Every brokerage firm shall deposit or place trust funds received into a neutral escrow
  depository or in a trust fund account with some bank or recognized depository, which is federally
  insured, by the next business day following their receipt[.]" The depository must be located in the
  same state (Hawaii) where the property is located.
- (e) Personal property (non-cash trust property) held for others must likewise be safeguarded in a
  secure in-State place by the next business day.
- (f) Records of personal property held in trust (non-funds) must be retained for at least 3 years,
  subject to Commission inspection, and must document: date of receipt, description/type of property,
  from whom received, for whose benefit held, and date/to whom delivered.
- (g) Trust property received by a salesperson/broker-salesperson must be delivered to their
  supervising principal broker/broker-in-charge by the next business day, unless otherwise instructed
  in writing by the depositor.
- (h)-(i) Principal broker/broker-in-charge may not commingle client funds with other moneys
  (commingling includes retaining earned commissions/fees in the client trust account beyond a
  reasonable time); interest accrued on trust deposits must be disbursed per a written agreement signed
  by the fund owner, the principal broker/broker-in-charge, and other parties to the transaction, or
  else paid to the fund owner.
=> Key numeric rule for question-writing: trust funds/property must be deposited/delivered "by the
next business day" following receipt (not 24 hours, not 3 banking days — the Hawaii-specific standard
is explicitly "next business day").

## CONFIRMED FACT 7: Continuing Education (CE) requirement for active-status renewal
Source: https://cca.hawaii.gov/reb/faqs-re_faqs/ (REB Licensing/Renewals/Education FAQ), exact quote:
"All individual licensees (except for those who received their salesperson license in 2026) desiring
to renew their licenses on an active status shall complete mandatory core courses, Parts A and B
(three (3) hours each), and fourteen (14) hours of elective credit hours." => Total 20 CE hours per
renewal cycle (6 hrs mandatory core [Part A + Part B, 3 hrs each] + 14 hrs elective), all courses must
be from the HI Real Estate Commission's approved list. Failure to complete CE results in renewal on
inactive status only (not automatic license loss). Inactive-status renewal does not require CE.
Also found: license-restoration CE makeup rule — forfeited <1 year: must show CE completion (active) /
no CE needed (inactive); forfeited 1-4 years: must do 30 hrs elective CE, OR retake the prelicense
course for that license level, OR (a third option was cut off in the fetched excerpt — not confirmed).
